How to use this dashboard

RuView reads how people disturb the WiFi signals around them. This page shows whether someone is here, whether they're moving, and whether your setup is healthy. With extra sensors, it also shows where they are.

Why it's useful: no cameras or wearables. It works in the dark and through walls, and nothing leaves your computer.

1Start it

In PowerShell, run:

powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File C:\RuViewDashboard\start-ruview.ps1

Keep that window open. Press Ctrl+C in it to stop.

Why: the script starts RuView (which reads your WiFi) and this dashboard together. They share a fresh secret token, so you never have to handle it.

2Check the badge (top left)

  • LIVE: real data from your WiFi
  • DEMO: made-up practice data, because RuView isn't running
  • STALE: data stopped arriving; run the script again
Why: the page never pretends. If it says DEMO, nothing shown is about your room.

3Read the Health checklist

✓ green = working · ! yellow = works with limits · ✕ red = something to fix · – grey = needs extra hardware

Each line says what to do if it isn't green.

Why: it tells you whether a number can be trusted before you rely on it.

4Draw your place (one time)

  1. Pick a floor tab. Use + Floor to add another, and double-click a tab (or use Rename floor) to name it.
  2. Click Clear floor to remove that floor's sample rooms.
  3. Set W and H to your home's size in meters (1 square = 1 m).
  4. Click + Room, drag a box, and type a name. Repeat for each room.
  5. Click + Router and click where your router is. Do the same with + Laptop.
  6. If you get ESP32 boards later, add each with + ESP32 node.

With Move on, click a room to select it. Then drag it to move it, drag a corner square to resize it, double-click it to rename it, or press Delete to remove it. Your plan saves automatically.

Why: WiFi only knows signals. The plan turns "node 2 sees movement" into "you're in the kitchen."

5Find the person on the plan

The purple figure shows who was sensed. A red number on it means several people are detected.

  • Laptop WiFi only: a circle about 5 m around your laptop, labeled "somewhere in this circle." The laptop knows someone is nearby, but not where.
  • 1–2 ESP32 boards: the figure stands near the board that sees the most movement, and that room is highlighted.
  • 3+ ESP32 boards: the figure moves to your estimated spot (±1.5 m) and shows which room you're in.
Why the limit: a position needs measurements from 3+ different spots, the way a phone's GPS uses several satellites. Your laptop is one spot and only measures signal strength, so it can't tell where you are.

6The other panels

  • People / Motion: the most reliable readings on laptop WiFi.
  • Breathing / Heart: filled in only once RuView trusts them. Laptop WiFi is too coarse to see breathing, so they stay blank until you add an ESP32 sensor. These are not medical readings.
  • Signal waterfall: each row is a WiFi signal, with color showing its strength over time. Movement appears as streaks.
  • Pose: needs ESP32 data, and even then it's a rough placeholder.
  • Event log: what changed and when.

7Get better results

  • Use it at home, not in a café. Other people's movement confuses the readings.
  • Keep the laptop still, with you between it and the router if you can.
  • Wait 30 seconds after starting. RuView first learns what the room looks like.
